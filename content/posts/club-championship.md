There's a version of yourself that only shows up under pressure. 

I met mine on the first hole of the club championship — a straightforward 380-yard par four with nothing to fear except the people watching from the cart path. I hit driver 30 yards left, punched out sideways, hit a 9-iron to 40 feet, and three-putted for double bogey. In five minutes, all the progress I'd made over six months felt like fiction.

This is what competition does. It burns away everything but the truth.

## The Pressure Experiment

I'd been shooting in the mid-to-high 80s in casual rounds for two months. My handicap was sitting at 13.9. I genuinely believed I might contend. Walking to the first tee, I told myself this was just another round.

It wasn't another round.

My heart rate was elevated for the first five holes. I kept looking at other players' scores. I checked the app twice to calculate what I'd need to shoot to finish in the top five. All the mental habits that ruin rounds — I fell into every one of them.

By the turn I was 9-over. The tournament was effectively over.

## The Back Nine Discovery

Something interesting happens when the stakes are gone. Freed from the pressure of the leaderboard, I played the back nine in 3-over. Three pars in a row on 11, 12, and 13. A birdie on 15 that made me pump my fist embarrassingly in front of a foursome I didn't know.

The back nine showed me what I'm capable of when my nervous system isn't fighting me.

## What I Learned

**The mental game is not a soft skill.** It is the hardest skill. Every person in that field could hit the shots. The separation was entirely psychological — who could access their ability under load and who couldn't.

**Prepare for the first tee.** I arrived ten minutes before my tee time. I hit five wedges and called it warm. Next year: forty-five minutes minimum, full pre-round routine, putts on the practice green.

**Compete more often.** The only way to learn how to handle pressure is to put yourself under it repeatedly. I've entered two more stroke play events this summer. I expect to embarrass myself. That's the work.

> Pressure is a privilege. It means you're doing something that matters.

I finished 12th. Next year I'm coming for the top half.
