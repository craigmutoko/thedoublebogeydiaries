There's a particular cruelty to the first round of a new season. You've spent the winter watching tournament footage, reading instruction manuals, regripping irons in the garage. You arrive at the first tee convinced — *convinced* — that the work has paid off. Then the ball goes 40 yards right into a stand of eucalyptus.

## The Opening Holes

I teed off just past 7 a.m. on a Tuesday, the marine layer still draped over the canyon. Torrey Pines North is my home course in every sense that matters — I know its slopes, its optical illusions, the way the third green breaks hard left even when it looks straight. None of this knowledge helped on hole one.

My driver, which I'd been convinced was fixed, opened up like a door. Ball found the scrub. Took a drop. Made double. Classic season opener.

> "Golf is deceptively simple and endlessly complicated; it satisfies the soul and frustrates the intellect. It is at the same time rewarding and maddening — and it is without doubt the greatest game mankind has ever invented." — Arnold Palmer

By the turn I'd settled in, posting a 47 on the front nine. Not brilliant, but the short game was sharp — six one-putts and no three-putts. That's the thing about winter putting practice in the living room: it actually transfers.

## The Back Nine Saves It

The back nine at Torrey North asks different questions. The par fives are reachable in two if you're striping it, and by hole eleven I was striping it. Back nine came in at 43. Gross 90. Handicap differential: 16.4.

Fine. I'll take it.

## What to Work On

The driver needs a reset. My takeaway is too flat in the early season — a mechanical problem that compounds when I'm not warmed up. Three things to address before the next round:

1. Slow down the transition. The first move down is where I'm losing everything.
2. Get back to the range twice a week minimum.
3. Stop overthinking the par threes. They're par threes. Hit the ball.
